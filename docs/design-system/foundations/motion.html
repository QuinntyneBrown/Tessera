Foundation

Motion

Motion in Tessera is quiet and functional: it confirms that a control responded, shows where something came from, and reports that work is still happening. It never decorates, and it steps aside when a learner asks for less of it.

Overview

Every transition and animation in components.css reads a duration token and an easing token; nothing hard-codes milliseconds. That gives the system three properties:

  • Consistency. A hover on a button, a lesson in the SCORM outline and a combobox option all change colour over the same 150 ms.
  • One switch for reduced motion. tokens.css shortens every duration under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce, so components need no media queries of their own for transitions.
  • Status loops stay honest. The spinner, the LIVE dot and the skeleton shimmer have their own loop tokens so they can be treated differently from transitions: the spinner slows down instead of stopping, because it reports that work is still happening.

The demos on this page replay with their Play button. If your system asks for reduced motion, the dots jump instead of gliding: that is the token doing its job.

Durations

Pick a duration by how far something travels and how much of the screen it changes. Small, local feedback is fast; anything that changes the layout of the view is slower so the eye can follow it.

TokenValueUseDemo
--duration-instantState changes that must not lag: selected option, pressed toggle, chip removal.
--duration-fastHover and active colours on buttons, fields, options, lesson links; the big play button pop; switch thumb.
--duration-baseThe video control bar fading in and out; toasts leaving; popups appearing.
--duration-slowDialogs and bottom sheets when they animate; the SCORM outline drawer on phones.
--duration-deliberateProgress bars filling (course completion, upload progress), so a jump from 40% to 75% reads as progress, not a glitch.

Durations stop at 500 ms. Nothing a learner waits for should be animated longer than that; if a change needs more time, it is a loading state, not a transition.

Easings

Easing says where motion is going. Each demo below runs over --duration-deliberate so the curve is easy to see.

TokenValueUseDemo
--ease-standardThe default for anything that changes in place: colour, border, opacity, progress width. Same curve as @tessera/theme curveEasyEase.
--ease-enterThings arriving: popups, toasts, dialogs, the big play button. Starts fast, settles gently.
--ease-exitThings leaving: a toast fading, a popup closing. Starts slowly, then gets out of the way.
--ease-springA small overshoot for one affirmative moment, such as a switch thumb landing. Never for large surfaces or anything that carries text.
--ease-linearLoops only: spinner rotation and skeleton shimmer. Linear transitions look mechanical, so never use it for a one-off change.

Choreography and usage

Rules

  • Animate opacity, colour and transform only. Never animate width, height, top or margins; they reflow text at 200% and stutter on low-end devices. The progress bar width is the single exception, over --duration-deliberate.
  • Enter with --ease-enter, leave with --ease-exit, and leave one step faster than you arrived (a popup that opens over --duration-base closes over --duration-fast).
  • One thing moves at a time. When a dialog opens, the backdrop and the dialog share one duration; nothing else on the page animates. Lists do not stagger their items in.
  • Motion follows cause. A popup grows from its field, a toast rises from the edge it sits on, the video control bar slides 4px (--space-1) from the bottom it is attached to.
  • Never block input. A transition must not delay focus or activation: focus moves to a dialog's heading immediately, and the SCORM outline is operable while it opens.
  • Loops end with their state. A spinner disappears the moment results arrive, and a skeleton is replaced, not faded. Nothing loops forever on an idle page except the LIVE dot, which is itself the state.
  • No flashing. Nothing flashes more than three times a second (WCAG 2.3.1); the pulse is 1.6 s and fades only to 35% opacity.

Writing a transition

.thing {
  transition: opacity var(--duration-base) var(--ease-enter),
              transform var(--duration-base) var(--ease-enter);
}
.thing[data-leaving="true"] {
  opacity: 0;
  transition-duration: var(--duration-fast);
  transition-timing-function: var(--ease-exit);
}

Reduced motion

Tessera follows WCAG 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions even though it is AAA: motion is never required to understand or operate anything, and all of it can be switched off.

  • Transitions: under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce, tokens.css sets --duration-fast, --duration-base, --duration-slow and --duration-deliberate to 0.01ms. The change still happens, instantly. A near-zero value (rather than 0) keeps transitionend events firing for scripts that wait on them.
  • Status loop: --duration-loop-spin becomes 2000ms. The spinner keeps turning, slowly, because a frozen spinner looks like a hang (WCAG 2.2.2 allows essential status animation).
  • Decorative loops: --duration-loop-pulse and --duration-loop-shimmer drop to 0.01ms, and components.css also sets animation: none on skeletons, the LIVE dot, the big play button and indeterminate progress, so nothing flickers.
  • Transforms: the 1px press translate on buttons is removed.
  • Scrolling: smooth scrolling is turned off (scroll-behavior: auto).
  • Shipped packages: @tessera/theme sets durationNormal to 0ms and the video player's --t-video-player-motion-duration falls back to it; see theming for how the two systems line up.

To check: turn on "Reduce motion" (macOS, iOS), "Show animations: off" (Windows) or "Remove animations" (Android), or emulate prefers-reduced-motion in Chromium DevTools, then replay the demos above. Record emulated runs as emulated.
