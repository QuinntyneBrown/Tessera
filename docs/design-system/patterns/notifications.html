Pattern

Notifications

A notification tells learners and administrators about something they did not just look at: a save that failed, an assignment that went out, a stream that came back. Choose the quietest form that still reaches everyone, and never let a message disappear before it can be read.

Overview

Tessera has four notification surfaces, from loudest to quietest:

  • Banner (.alert--banner): a page-wide condition that persists until it is resolved, at the top of the page or region.
  • Inline alert (.alert): a condition scoped to one region, placed at the top of that region, with its recovery action. SCORM "Progress not saved" is one.
  • Toast (.toast): a short-lived confirmation of a finished task, in a fixed region at the edge of the viewport, optionally with one action such as Undo.
  • Live region only: words for assistive technology when the change is already visible where the learner is looking: "Captions on.", "Back live.", "6 results available."

Tessera has no notification centre: the components live inside a host LMS, which owns its inbox. Which feedback answers which action is covered in feedback and loading; the component details are in toast, alert and inline message.

Priority

  1. Blocking error: the learner's work is at risk ("Progress not saved"). Inline alert, role="alert", persists, moves focus only if the failing surface had focus.
  2. Warning: something will go wrong if ignored ("Exit without saving?"). Inline alert, persists.
  3. Success or info: confirmation of finished work. Toast or live region.
  4. Ambient status: state changes the learner caused or can see. Live region only.

A higher priority never waits behind a lower one. A new failure replaces any pending polite status in the same component; it is not queued after it.

Grouping and coalescing

  • Video player: messages arriving within 150 ms are coalesced into one update of the polite region. Toggle announcements ("Muted.", "Captions on.") queue and are all spoken; status announcements ("Buffering.", "Live.") replace any earlier pending status. A toggle is never dropped.
  • SCORM player: messages from one task are joined, so a later one never overwrites an earlier one unread: "Put it into practice complete. Course complete. Progress saved."
  • Combobox: a failure discards any pending status ("Results could not be loaded." is not queued behind "Loading results.").
  • Toasts: at most three visible; repeats of the same message update the existing toast with a count ("3 learners assigned") instead of stacking. The oldest non-error toast leaves first.
  • An unchanged status is never re-announced (SCORM does not repeat a completion), and a closed alert is emptied so it does not replay old text when reopened.

Responsive behaviour

  • The toast region spans the viewport minus 16px margins below --layout-breakpoint-sm, above the safe-area inset; above it, it sits bottom-right at 24rem wide.
  • Toasts never cover the SCORM Previous / Next row or the video controls on phones: the host offsets the region above sticky footers.
  • Banners use --layout-margin inline padding so they align with page content; actions wrap below the text at narrow widths.
  • Below 768px the video control bar moves under the picture, and the "Back live" pill moves to the bottom of the stage.
  • At 200% text, toasts grow taller rather than truncating; long text is a sign the message belongs in an inline alert.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

The WAI-ARIA APG Alert pattern for failures, and polite live regions for everything else (WCAG 4.1.3 Status Messages). Live regions exist and are empty on first render, and are filled later; a region inserted with its text already in it is often not read. Auto-dismiss honours 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable by pausing on hover and focus and by never timing out errors.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabReaches toast actions and Dismiss in DOM order; the toast region sits at the end of the host page. Focus pauses every toast timer.
Enter, SpaceActivates Undo, Retry or Dismiss.
EscWith focus in a toast, dismisses that toast and returns focus to where it came from.

Focus

Notifications do not take focus, with one exception from the SCORM mock: when an exit attempt reveals unsaved progress, focus moves to the "Progress not saved" heading because the learner must decide. A closing alert or toast that held focus returns it to a sensible element (the activity heading in SCORM), never to <body>.

Labelling

Dismiss buttons name what they dismiss when more than one is on screen ("Dismiss maintenance notice"). Icons are aria-hidden; the title carries the severity in words ("Progress not saved", not a red icon alone).

Motion

Toasts fade over --duration-base with --ease-exit on leave; no sliding. Under prefers-reduced-motion they appear and disappear instantly. Alerts never animate.

Touch

Toast buttons are 32px high with 8px spacing on a toast that is itself well over 44px; banner and alert actions are 44px. Toasts do not capture swipes; Dismiss is always a visible button.

Content

  • Titles state the outcome in sentence case: "Progress not saved", "Assignment sent", "No access to this stream".
  • Bodies follow the error formula: what happened, what is safe, what to do. "Your latest progress is kept on this page. Try saving again."
  • One message, one action. The action is a verb: "Retry save", "Undo", "Sign in again".
  • Spoken status is short and ends with a full stop: "Back live.", "Captions on.", "Course exited."
  • Name people and courses, not ids: "Grace Hopper could not be assigned."
