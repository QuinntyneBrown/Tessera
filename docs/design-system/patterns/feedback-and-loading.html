Pattern

Feedback and loading

Every action gets an answer at the place and weight it deserves: an inline message beside the field, a toast for a finished background task, a banner for a page-wide condition, a dialog only when the learner must decide. While work is in progress, the shape of the wait chooses the indicator: skeleton, spinner or progress bar.

Overview

Feedback has two jobs: show that the system heard the action, and say how it ended. Tessera's three mocks each solve one of these well, and this pattern generalises them.

  • Acknowledge within 100 ms. Visual state changes immediately: the combobox spinner appears in the field as soon as a request starts; the video player shows its connecting skeleton the moment it mounts.
  • Announce only what lasts. Screen reader announcements of a wait are delayed by 1 s, so fast responses never produce "Loading… 6 results available" chatter. The combobox says "Loading results." after 1 s; the video player says "Buffering." only after buffering has lasted 1 s.
  • End every wait with an outcome. Success, empty or failure, each in words: "6 results available.", "No results found.", "Results could not be loaded."
  • Keep what the learner did. A failure never discards input or selections: "Learners could not be loaded. Your selections are safe." "Your latest progress is kept on this page."

For the components themselves see skeleton, spinner, progress bar, inline message, alert and toast. How messages are prioritised and dismissed is in notifications; what to say when there is nothing or something broke is in empty and error states.

Optimistic updates and undo

Update the UI before the server confirms when the action is reversible, very likely to succeed, and cheap to roll back: selecting a learner, removing a chip, toggling captions. Offer Undo in a toast for removals that cannot be redone with one click. Do not update optimistically when the result is the learner's record of achievement (SCORM completion and saves): the SCORM player shows "Last saved at 10:42" only after the save succeeded, and "Not saved" otherwise. Completion comes only from the content, never from player chrome.

Delayed announcements

The visual indicator and the spoken one run on different clocks. Show the spinner at once; start a 1 s timer for the announcement; cancel the timer if the work finishes first. When it finishes, announce the outcome either way.

// Combobox (mock): spinner now, words after 1 s, outcome always
spinner.hidden = false;
loadingTimer = setTimeout(() => announce('Loading results.'), 1000);
onResponse(() => { clearTimeout(loadingTimer); announce(n ? `${n} results available.` : 'No results found.'); });

// Video (mock): buffering spinner now, "Buffering." only if it lasts 1 s
later(() => { if (state === 'buffering') announce('Buffering.'); }, 1000);

Responsive behaviour

  • Skeletons take the exact box of the content they stand for (the video stage keeps 16:9), so nothing shifts when it arrives at any width.
  • Spinners stay inside their component: in the combobox input row, in the video stage, in a busy button. Never a full-page spinner over content that is still usable.
  • Progress text wraps under the label at 320px rather than truncating; the bar keeps full width.
  • Toasts span the viewport minus 16px margins below --layout-breakpoint-sm and sit bottom-right at 24rem wide above it, clear of the safe-area inset.
  • At 200% text and 400% zoom, status rows wrap ("Learners could not be loaded. Your selections are safe." then Retry on its own line); they never clip.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Status messages reach assistive technology without moving focus (WCAG 4.1.3 Status Messages). Each component owns one polite live region (aria-live="polite" aria-atomic="true") that exists, empty, from first render; failures that block the learner use role="alert". The progress bar uses role="progressbar" with aria-valuetext. Spinners and skeletons are aria-hidden; the region that is loading may set aria-busy="true".

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabReaches Retry, Undo and Dismiss in DOM order. Loading never removes the focused element; a busy button keeps focus.
EnterIn the combobox, retries a failed load or loads more when no option is active.
EscCloses the combobox popup; does not cancel a save. Long waits offer a visible Cancel (SCORM "Loading course" has Cancel).

Focus

Feedback does not steal focus. The exceptions follow the mocks: when a blocking error replaces the surface that had focus, focus moves to the error heading (video error panel, SCORM "Progress not saved" on exit); when loading replaces the course while focus was inside it, focus moves to the "Loading course" heading and that is the announcement. Otherwise use the polite region. A closing region never strands focus on <body>.

Labelling

Indicators are named by text next to them, never by the animation: "Loading learners…", "Connecting…", "2 of 4 activities complete". Retry buttons say what they retry when context is not obvious ("Retry save").

Motion

Under prefers-reduced-motion skeleton shimmer stops (flat --color-skeleton), the indeterminate bar becomes a static half-opacity track, and spinners keep turning but slow to 2 s per revolution, because they report status. Progress width changes over --duration-deliberate, zeroed under reduced motion.

Touch

Retry, Undo and Dismiss are at least 32px with 8px spacing inside toasts and status rows, whose rows are themselves larger targets; standalone recovery buttons are 44px.

Content

  • Waiting text names the thing and ends with an ellipsis: "Loading learners…", "Connecting…", "Checking the course package…". Spoken versions end with a full stop: "Loading results."
  • Outcomes are short past-tense or state sentences: "Progress saved.", "Back live.", "3 more results loaded."
  • Failures follow the formula: what happened, what is safe, what to do. "Learners could not be loaded. Your selections are safe." with Retry.
  • Undo toasts name the object: "Ada Lovelace removed from the assignment." Button: "Undo".
  • Never "Please wait", "Oops" or "Success!".
