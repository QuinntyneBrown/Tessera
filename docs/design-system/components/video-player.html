Video player

The live video player streams a lecture or session (Lecture hall A, week 7 of Thermal transfer) with a slim, keyboard-first control bar. It is live only: no seek bar, pause holds the picture, and resume jumps back to the live edge.

Overview

Use the video player for a live stream inside a course or session page: a lecture camera, a live demonstration, a webinar. It owns connection, buffering and reconnection feedback, captions and the control bar, so the host page only supplies the stream and a token.

  • Use it for live streams delivered by the host's hub (fragmented MP4 over SignalR). Recorded video with a seek bar is a different component and is not part of Tessera yet.
  • Don't use it for decorative or muted background video, or for SCORM content: course packages play in the SCORM player.
  • Transport controls (.media-ctl) are not general buttons: they always sit on the dark media scrim and use the media focus ring. Errors inside the player reuse the alert, transient status reuses a pill toast, and waiting reuses the on-media spinner and skeleton.

Anatomy

  1. Region.media-player, role="region" named "Video player: Lecture hall A". Black, --radius-lg corners, clips its children.
  2. Stage.media-player__stage: 16:9 box holding the <video> (contained, never cropped). Not in the Tab sequence; a click or tap toggles playback.
  3. Overlays.media-player__overlay (--dim, --skeleton), the big play button, the Unmute chip, the "Back live" pill toast, the ended card and the error panel. One state overlay at a time.
  4. CaptionsA real <track kind="captions"> styled with ::cue; drawn here with .media-player__caption. Sits above the bar while the bar is visible.
  5. Play / Pause.media-ctl, 44px, filled icon; the label flips between "Play" and "Pause".
  6. MuteToggle with aria-pressed; the icon shows high, low or off volume.
  7. VolumeNative range, 0–100 in steps of 5, labelled "Volume" with aria-valuetext="60%". Hidden below 48rem.
  8. LIVE badge.media-ctl.media-live: pulsing .media-live__dot, "LIVE" and .media-live__elapsed (aria-hidden). A button that is aria-disabled at the live edge.
  9. CaptionsToggle with aria-pressed, after .media-player__spacer; only rendered when a track is supplied.
  10. FullscreenToggle with aria-pressed; label becomes "Exit fullscreen". Only rendered when fullscreen is supported.

Not shown above, all inside the region: the Unmute chip (.media-player__chip, the accent pill used when autoplay starts muted; going back to live is done with the LIVE badge, not a chip), the ended card (.media-player__card), the error panel (.media-player__error with an .alert.alert--danger), the waiting spinner (.spinner.spinner--lg.spinner--on-media) and one visually hidden polite live region.

Variants

The player has one look; what varies is the scenario. Each scenario below matches a state of the shipped player (data-state on the host is idle, connecting, live, buffering, paused, reconnecting, ended or error) and a scenario in the mock. The stage is left empty here; in the product it shows the picture.

Sizes

One size, fluid. The region fills its container's width and the stage keeps a 16:9 aspect-ratio, so the height follows the width; the picture is letterboxed (object-fit: contain) when the source is not 16:9. Controls never scale: every target stays 44px (--target-comfortable), so at narrow widths the bar wraps or moves below the picture instead of shrinking.

The same player in 20rem and 32rem containers. Width comes from the host layout; give the player a column, not a fixed height.

Fullscreen makes the region fill the screen; the stage stays centred and letterboxed.

  • Hover fills the control with --media-hover (white at 14%). The big play button turns accent with dark ink.
  • Focus draws the 3px --color-media-focus-ring (amber 300) with a 2px offset; it is lighter than the page ring so it reads on the dark scrim.
  • Active has no separate treatment: it keeps the hover fill while pressed.
  • Pressed (Mute, Captions, Fullscreen) adds a 2px accent underline and swaps the icon; the name changes only for Mute ("Unmute") and Fullscreen ("Exit fullscreen").
  • Disabled uses aria-disabled="true" so the control stays focusable and announces as unavailable; it dims to 55%. The volume slider uses native disabled after the stream ends.
  • LIVE at the edge is aria-disabled but keeps full opacity and a pulsing red dot: it is a status first. Behind it becomes an enabled button named "Go to live, 14 seconds behind", gains an accent outline and a neutral dot; Enter or Space jumps to live.

Both themes

Controls always sit on a dark scrim over the picture, so the media tokens barely change between themes: dark deepens the scrim (82% to 88%) and softens the ink to sage 25. The connecting skeleton and the error panel follow the page theme.

Responsive

  • 48rem and wider: the control bar overlays the bottom of the picture on a gradient scrim and fades out 3 s after the pointer rests while playing. It never hides while a control has focus, and hidden controls use a class, never aria-hidden, so Tab still reaches and reveals them.
  • Below 48rem (--layout-breakpoint-md): the bar sits below the picture on a solid scrim, so a wrapped two-row bar never covers the video or the captions; it does not auto-hide; the volume slider is hidden (use the device volume or ↑/↓); the "Back live" pill moves to the bottom of the stage.
  • Touch: on hidden controls the first tap only reveals them; the next tap toggles playback.
  • Zoom and text spacing: at 400% zoom (320 CSS px) the player takes the bar-below layout, controls wrap, and nothing scrolls horizontally. The error panel and ended card wrap their text inside the stage.
Below 48rem: bar below the picture, solid scrim, no volume slider (simulated here with inline styles so it shows at any width).

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A role="region" named "Video player: {title}" containing a role="group" named "Player controls" of native buttons and a native range input. It is a group, not a toolbar, because arrow keys are reserved for volume. Toggles follow the APG Button pattern with aria-pressed; volume follows the APG Slider pattern through the native input. Errors use role="alert".

Keyboard

Tab order: Play/Pause, Mute, Volume (hidden below 48rem), LIVE, Captions (when supplied), Fullscreen (when supported). The stage is not in the Tab sequence. Shortcuts work whenever focus is inside the player; letters ignore Ctrl, Alt and Meta.

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves through the controls; focusing a hidden bar reveals it. aria-disabled controls stay in the sequence.
Space or KPlay or pause from anywhere in the player except the volume slider. Space on a focused button activates that button instead. Resume jumps to the live edge.
Enter / Space on a controlActivates it. On LIVE while behind: jumps to live.
MMute or unmute.
↑ / ↓Volume up or down by 5 from anywhere in the player. On the slider the native arrow behaviour applies and the player does not add a second step.
CCaptions on or off.
FEnter or exit fullscreen.
EscExits fullscreen.

Focus

Controls use the media ring: 3px --color-media-focus-ring at 2px offset, on :focus-visible. When an error or the ended state appears while focus is inside the player, focus moves to the panel's heading (tabindex="-1"); after Retry it moves to Play. Hiding the bar never moves or strands focus.

Labelling and announcements

Every control has an aria-label; icons are aria-hidden. The elapsed clock is aria-hidden because it changes every second. One polite live region per player announces milestones and toggles; status messages within 150 ms coalesce (the last one wins), toggle messages are never dropped.

Motion

The bar fades over --duration-base, the big play button pops over --duration-fast, the LIVE dot pulses over --duration-loop-pulse and the skeleton shimmers over --duration-loop-shimmer. Under prefers-reduced-motion durations are zeroed, the pulse, shimmer and pop stop, and the spinner keeps turning slowly (2 s) because it reports status. Nothing flashes.

Forced colours

In Windows High Contrast the scrim becomes Canvas, ink ButtonText, accent and the LIVE dot Highlight (the dot opts out of adjustment so it stays visible), the bar gets a ButtonText top border and every control, the big play button and the chip get a 1px ButtonText border. The focus ring becomes Highlight.

Touch

Every control, the chip and the LIVE badge are at least 44 × 44px; the big play button is 72px. The first tap on hidden controls reveals them rather than toggling playback. Hover fills only appear where the pointer can hover.

Content

  • Control names are short verbs or nouns in sentence case: "Play", "Pause", "Mute", "Unmute", "Captions", "Fullscreen", "Exit fullscreen". The visual "LIVE" badge is uppercase with --letter-spacing-wide; its accessible name is "Live".
  • Status lines end with an ellipsis character while work continues ("Connecting…", "Reconnecting… attempt 2 of 5", "Waiting for the source…") and with a full stop when announced as a sentence ("Buffering.", "Back live.").
  • Times are hh:mm:ss with tabular numbers on screen ("Live for 00:48:12"), spoken as words ("It was live for 48 minutes").
  • Error messages say what happened and what to do, without codes: "You don't have access to this stream. Sign in again or ask the organiser." Technical detail (the MIME type) appears only where it helps support, as in the unsupported message.
  • Use the session's real title ("Lecture hall A") in the region name and connecting line; set titleOverride when the stream descriptor's title is not learner-friendly. All strings are localisable through VIDEO_PLAYER_I18N.

Code

In an Angular app, use the shipped component; it renders the structure below and owns every state.

<t-video-player
  hubUrl="https://lms.example/hubs/video"
  streamId="lecture-hall-a"
  [accessTokenFactory]="token"
  [captions]="{ src: '/captions/lecture-hall-a.vtt', srclang: 'en', label: 'English' }"
  (stateChange)="state = $event"
  (error)="report($event)"
/>
