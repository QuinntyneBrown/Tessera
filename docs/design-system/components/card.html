Card

A card is a bordered surface that holds everything about one subject: a course, a live session, a lesson outline, the notes beside a player. It separates that subject from the page without adding meaning of its own.

Overview

Use a card for a self-contained unit that sits beside others or beside a main panel: a course in a catalogue grid, the "Try the interaction" notes beside a player, the SCORM course outline. Every mock uses this surface: a white (dark: sage-900) panel with a hairline --color-border-default edge and 12px corners.

  • Default: static content with a heading.
  • Interactive: the whole card is clickable through one real link in its heading, stretched over the card. The card itself is never the control.
  • With header and footer: a header row with title and meta or a badge, a footer row with actions above a divider.
  • Media: a 16:9 image or poster flush with the top edge.
  • Selected: accent edge and fill driven by a checkbox inside the card.

Do not use a card for a single line of text (use a list), for a status message (use an alert), or to wrap a whole page. Do not nest cards.

Anatomy

  1. Container.card: --color-bg-surface, 1px --color-border-default, --radius-lg (12px). No shadow at rest.
  2. Header.card__header: heading (--text-h4) and an optional badge or action; wraps; 20px padding, 12px below.
  3. Body.card__body: 20px padding (--space-5); top padding drops after a header. Secondary text uses .card__eyebrow.
  4. Footer.card__footer: hairline top divider, 16 × 20px padding, wrapping row with meta at the start and actions at the end.

Media is decorative (aria-hidden) unless it carries information; selected state comes from the checkbox inside.

Sizes

Cards have one padding scale (20px, from the mocks' panels). Their width comes from the layout (grid columns or the wrapping sidebar layout), not from a size modifier.

States

Only interactive and selectable cards have states. Hover and focus are forced with data-state; in products focus comes from :has(.card__link:focus-visible), so the ring is drawn around the whole card while DOM focus stays on the link.

Responsive behaviour

  • Cards fill their grid track. Catalogue grids use .grid--auto (minmax(min(100%, 18rem), 1fr)): one column on phones, as many 18rem columns as fit above.
  • Panel cards beside a main area use the wrapping sidebar layout: the side card stacks above the main one as soon as the main cannot keep 30rem, as the SCORM outline and the notes panels do.
  • Header and footer rows wrap: the badge drops under the title and actions under the meta rather than squeezing. Text never truncates inside a card; titles wrap.
  • Below --layout-breakpoint-md the mocks reduced panel padding to 16px; cards keep 20px, which still leaves 248px of content at 320px.
  • Media keeps 16:9 at every width and scales with the card.

Theming

The card reads --card-bg (--color-bg-surface) and --card-border (--color-border-default); hover sets the border to --color-border-strong, selected sets both to the accent pair. A host changes card surfaces by overriding these two tokens.

/* Cards on a sunken host page */
.host-dashboard .card { --card-bg: var(--color-bg-surface-raised); }

In the dark theme the surface is --palette-sage-900 on a --palette-sage-950 canvas with a --palette-sage-700 edge; elevation comes from the lighter surface, not the shadow. In the shipped packages these are --t-colorNeutralBackground1 and --t-colorNeutralStroke1; see theming.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

No widget role. Use <article> for a self-contained item in a collection (a course) and <section> or <aside> for a panel (notes, outline), labelled by its heading via aria-labelledby. A collection of cards is a <ul> when the count matters. The interactive card follows the APG Link pattern: one real link in the heading, stretched over the card with a pseudo-element.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabReaches the card's link once, then any other control inside the card (checkbox, footer buttons) in DOM order.
EnterFollows the card's link.
SpaceToggles the selection checkbox when it has focus.

Focus

The ring is drawn around the card (outline on .card--interactive:has(.card__link:focus-visible)) while the link keeps DOM focus, so screen readers announce the link name and sighted keyboard users see the whole target. Buttons inside an interactive card must sit above the stretched link (position: relative; z-index: var(--z-raised)) or they cannot be clicked.

Labelling

The link text is the card's title, so the accessible name is short ("Better conversations at work, link"). Do not wrap the whole card in <a>: it makes the name the entire card text. Badges and meta follow the link in reading order and are read as plain text. Selection checkboxes reference the card heading with aria-describedby so "Assign this course" is announced with the course name.

The resting edge (--color-border-default) is decorative: the card is not a control, so 1.4.11 does not apply. Interactive cards are identified by their underlined-on-hover title and focus ring, not by the edge. Under prefers-contrast: more the edge becomes --color-border-strong.

Motion

Border colour and shadow transition over --duration-fast; reduced motion zeroes it. Cards never lift, scale or tilt on hover.

Touch

The whole interactive card is the target, far above 44px. Inner buttons are separate targets with at least 8px from the card edge. Hover styles are cosmetic and not needed to find the link.

Content

  • Lead with the title: the course or session name, in sentence case as the author wrote it.
  • Meta is one line of facts separated by middle dots: "Required learning · 35 minutes", "Lecture hall A · Dr. A. Okafor".
  • Status goes in a badge in the header ("In progress", "Complete", "Live"), never as coloured card edges alone.
  • Footer actions are verbs naming the next step: "Join session", "Resume lesson", "Review".
  • No "Read more" link: the title is the link.
