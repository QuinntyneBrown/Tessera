Divider

A divider is a hairline that separates groups of content: the course heading from the player, one session from the next, a cluster of toolbar actions from the rest. It organises; it does not carry meaning on its own.

Overview

Every mock separates regions with a 1px --color-border-default line: under the top bar, between the player bar and the content frame, above the player actions. Most of those lines belong to a component (.card__footer, .content-player__bar, .list--divided) and come with it. Use the divider component only when two groups sit in the same container with no other boundary.

  • Horizontal (.divider): between stacked groups, such as "Session details" and "Recordings" inside one panel.
  • Vertical (.divider--vertical): between clusters in a row, such as selection actions and "More actions" in a toolbar.
  • With label (.divider--label): a short overline label centred on the line, such as "Earlier sessions" in a list or "or" between two ways to do one thing.

Prefer white space (--space-6 to --space-8) or a heading over a line. Do not use a divider under every list item (use .list--divided), as a section heading's underline, or to box content (use a card).

Anatomy

  1. Line1px (--border-width-hairline) in --color-border-default. Decorative.
  2. Label (optional)--text-overline, uppercase, --letter-spacing-wide, --color-fg-muted; 12px gap to the lines on each side.
  3. Spacing16px above and below (--space-4) for horizontal; 8px either side for vertical.

Sizes

Dividers have one weight: a 1px hairline. Emphasis comes from spacing and headings, never from a thicker line.

States

Dividers are not interactive and have no states. Both themes are shown under Theming.

Responsive behaviour

  • Horizontal dividers span their container at every width.
  • Vertical dividers stretch to the row height (at least 24px). When a toolbar wraps onto two rows, the vertical divider would sit at a row end; hide it below --layout-breakpoint-md or move the trailing cluster into the "More actions" menu.
  • Labelled dividers keep their label on one line; the lines shrink. Keep labels to two or three words so they fit at 320px with 200% text.

Theming

The line reads --color-border-default: sage-100 in light, sage-700 in dark. Under prefers-contrast: more it becomes --color-border-strong; in forced colours it becomes CanvasText. In the shipped packages the same line is --t-colorNeutralStroke1; see theming.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

An <hr> is a thematic break with the implicit separator role; screen readers may announce it, so use it only where a break is meaningful. Lines that are purely decorative are CSS borders on a component, not <hr>. In a toolbar or menu a separator groups items and is expected. Labelled dividers expose their text; give the container role="separator" with an aria-label only when the label names the group that follows.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabSkips dividers; they are never focusable. (A focusable separator is a splitter, which this system does not provide.)

Focus

No focus. A divider must never sit so close to a control that it hides part of the control's focus ring; keep at least the 2px ring offset plus 3px clear.

Labelling

Plain <hr> needs no name. Label text is read as ordinary text. Do not put meaning only in the line ("everything below is optional"); say it in a heading or the label.

Contrast

Line on surface (decorative)
Line on page (decorative)
Label on surface

The line is decorative and exempt from 1.4.11; it is documented with a 1:1 floor so a regression to invisible is still caught. The label is text and meets 4.5:1.

Motion

None.

Touch

Not a target. Vertical dividers add 8px either side, which keeps neighbouring toolbar buttons apart.

Content

  • Labels are short nouns in sentence case, set uppercase by CSS: "Earlier sessions", "Completed lessons", "or".
  • Write the label in sentence case in the HTML; the uppercase is visual only, so screen readers do not spell it out.
  • If the label is really a heading for what follows, use a heading instead.
