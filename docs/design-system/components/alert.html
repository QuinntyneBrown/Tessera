Alert

An alert tells a learner or administrator about a condition that affects what they are doing right now: progress that was not saved, a stream that cannot play, a course that is still loading. It stays until the condition changes.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stablerole="alert" only for new, important content

Overview

Use an alert when something about the current view needs attention and will stay true until it is resolved: "Progress not saved", "No access to this stream", "Loading course". Alerts sit in the flow of the page, next to the thing they describe, and never cover content.

  • Inline alert (.alert) belongs to a region: above the SCORM player, inside the video player's error overlay, at the top of a form.
  • Page banner (.alert--banner) spans the page under the top bar for conditions that affect the whole product: scheduled maintenance, a read-only LMS period.
  • Use a toast instead for brief confirmation of something the person just did ("Progress saved"). Use an inline message for a single field. Use a dialog only when the person must decide before they can continue.
  • Use an empty state when a region has no content to show, and an error page when the whole page cannot load.

Announcement is a separate decision from appearance. An alert that appears in response to a failure (save failed, stream lost) is announced with role="alert" or by moving focus to its heading. An alert that is present when the page loads, or that reports routine status, uses role="status" or no role at all.

Anatomy

  1. Status stripe--border-width-stripe (4px) on the inline-start edge in the tone's solid colour. Mirrors in RTL.
  2. Icon20px, tone icon colour, aria-hidden="true". The icon shape differs per tone so colour is never the only cue.
  3. Title--text-h4 (bold 16px). A real heading at the right level in product (<h2> or <h3>), with tabindex="-1" when focus may move to it.
  4. Body--text-body; one or two sentences: what happened and what to do next.
  5. Actions (optional)One primary recovery action ("Retry save") and at most one secondary ("Exit without saving"). They wrap below the text when space runs out.
  6. Dismiss (optional)Ghost icon button, 44px, labelled "Dismiss: <title>". Only for alerts whose condition the person may ignore.

Variants

Tones

The tone says how serious the condition is. Neutral is the default for progress and waiting ("Loading course"); it uses the brand stripe on a plain surface, as the SCORM .notice does. Danger is for failures the person must act on.

With actions

Actions sit after the text and wrap below it on narrow containers. The recovery action is primary; the escape hatch is secondary. This is the SCORM save-error notice and the video player's error panel.

Progress not saved

Your latest progress is kept on this page. Try saving again.

No access to this stream

You don’t have access to this stream. Sign in again or ask the organiser.

The unsupported-codec error has no Retry, because retrying cannot help; it only explains.
HTML
<section class="alert alert--danger" aria-labelledby="save-error-title">
  <svg class="icon" aria-hidden="true" viewBox="0 0 24 24">…</svg>
  <div class="alert__content">
    <h3 class="alert__title" id="save-error-title" tabindex="-1">Progress not saved</h3>
    <p class="alert__body">Your latest progress is kept on this page. Try saving again.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="alert__actions">
    <button class="btn btn--primary" type="button">Retry save</button>
    <button class="btn" type="button">Exit without saving</button>
  </div>
  <p class="visually-hidden" role="alert"><!-- filled when the save fails --></p>
</section>

Dismissible

Only advisory alerts (info, success, some warnings) can be dismissed. Errors that block the task cannot; they go away when the condition is fixed.

Captions are available

Lecture hall A streams live captions. Turn them on with C or the Captions button.

After dismissal, focus moves to the next logical element (the region's heading), never to <body>.

Page banner

A banner is full width, square, and flush with the page edges; its inline padding follows --layout-margin so its text lines up with the page content. It drops the stripe and keeps the tone background and a bottom rule.

Scheduled maintenance on Saturday, 10 October, 22:00–23:00

Courses stay open, but progress saved during that hour may need to be saved again.

You are viewing the course as Grace Hopper

Progress is not recorded while you preview as a learner.

One banner at a time; stack the most severe first if two are unavoidable.
HTML
<div class="alert alert--warning alert--banner" role="region" aria-label="Service notice">
  <svg class="icon" aria-hidden="true" viewBox="0 0 24 24">…</svg>
  <div class="alert__content">
    <p class="alert__title">Scheduled maintenance on Saturday, 10 October, 22:00–23:00</p>
    <p class="alert__body">Courses stay open, but progress saved during that hour may need to be saved again.</p>
  </div>
</div>

Sizes

Alerts have one size; width comes from their container and a banner spans the page.

The alert container is not interactive; its states are the states of its action and dismiss buttons, plus the busy state while a retry runs. States are forced with data-state for review.

  • Appearing: the alert is inserted (or un-hidden) in place; it does not slide or fade. If it was caused by the person's own action and focus was inside the affected region, focus moves to its title (tabindex="-1"), as the video player does with "Something went wrong".
  • Retrying: the primary action shows its loading state (aria-busy="true"); the alert stays until the retry succeeds, then is removed and a polite status ("Progress saved.") is announced.
  • Dismissed: removed from the DOM; dismissal is remembered for the session where it makes sense (banners), never for errors.

Responsive behaviour

  • The alert is a wrapping flex row: content takes flex: 1 1 16rem, so when the container is narrower than about 16rem plus the actions, the actions drop below the text. Nothing truncates.
  • At 320px and at 400% zoom the icon, text and actions stack naturally; the dismiss button stays at the top inline-end corner with its 44px target.
  • Banners use --layout-margin for inline padding (16px on phones, 24px from 768px, 32px from 1024px), matching the page grid.
  • In the video player the error alert is centred over the picture with max-width: 36rem; below 768px the player keeps the same overlay and the alert reflows inside it.
  • With 200% text and WCAG text-spacing overrides the alert grows taller; it has no fixed height.

Theming

The alert reads five component tokens that each tone overrides: --alert-bg, --alert-fg, --alert-border, --alert-stripe and --alert-icon. Tones map to the status token families (--color-info-*, --color-success-*, --color-warning-*, --color-danger-*); neutral uses the surface and the accent.

/* An LMS host gives neutral notices its own stripe without touching .alert */
.course-shell .alert:not([class*="alert--"]) { --alert-stripe: var(--color-fg-accent); }

In the dark theme tone backgrounds become deep tints (--palette-red-950, --palette-blue-950…) and text lightens to the 200–300 steps, so body text stays above 7:1. Body text stays --color-fg-default in every tone; only the danger title takes --color-danger-fg. In the shipped packages the danger text corresponds to @tessera/theme's --t-colorPaletteRedForeground1; the theme has no status backgrounds yet, so tone tints come from the design-system tokens. See theming.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Follows the WAI-ARIA APG Alert pattern, applied sparingly:

  • New, important, caused now (save failed, stream error): role="alert" on the alert or on a visually hidden message inside it that is filled after insertion, so it is announced once. The SCORM mock fills a hidden role="alert" paragraph 50 ms after showing the notice; the video mock puts role="alert" on the error panel.
  • Routine status (loading, version notice): role="status", or no role when the alert is present at page load.
  • Banners: a role="region" with an aria-label so they are reachable as a landmark; no live role.
  • Wrap a titled inline alert in <section aria-labelledby> pointing at its title, as the SCORM notices do.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves through the alert's actions, then the dismiss button, in DOM order. The alert itself is not a tab stop.
Enter, SpaceActivates the focused action or dismisses.
EscNo effect. Alerts are part of the page, not overlays; Esc does not dismiss them.

Focus

Showing an alert does not move focus, except for a blocking error in a region the person was working in: then focus goes to the alert's title (tabindex="-1", ring on :focus-visible). After Retry succeeds, focus moves to the next sensible control (the video player moves it to Play); after dismissal, to the region heading.

Labelling

The title is a heading so screen reader users can find the alert by heading navigation. The icon is aria-hidden; the tone is carried by the words ("not saved", "No access"). The dismiss button's name includes the title: "Dismiss: Captions are available".

Motion

None. Alerts appear and disappear without animation, so nothing needs a reduced-motion alternative. A Retry button's spinner keeps turning under reduced motion, slowed to 2 s per revolution.

Touch

Action buttons are 44px (32px only in dense tables, with the row as a target); the dismiss button is a 44px icon button pulled into the corner with a negative margin so it does not add padding. Actions are at least 8px apart.

Content

  • Title: the condition in a few words, sentence case, no full stop: "Progress not saved", "No access to this stream", "Loading course".
  • Body: what happened in plain words, then what to do next: "Your latest progress is kept on this page. Try saving again." Reassure when nothing is lost.
  • Actions: verbs that name the recovery: "Retry save", "Retry", "Exit without saving". Never "OK".
  • Do not blame the person or expose internals; codec strings belong in a details line for support, as in "This browser can’t play this stream (video/mp4; codecs=…)".
  • Name the organiser, course or learner when it helps: "Ada Lovelace and 2 other learners have not started yet."
  • Banners include the date and time with time zone when they announce something scheduled.

Drift fixed during extraction:

  • Stripe width was 4px (SCORM notice) and 5px (video error panel); unified on --border-width-stripe (4px).
  • Panel title sizes 1rem (SCORM) and 1.1rem (video); normalised to --text-h4 for inline alerts.
  • Padding 0.9rem 1rem (SCORM) and 1rem 1.25rem (video); snapped to --space-4.
  • Body top margin 0.15rem; snapped to --space-0-5.
  • The SCORM error notice coloured all text red; body text now stays --color-fg-default and an icon is added so colour is not the only cue.
  • Video error button radius 7px; --radius-md through .btn.

Info, warning, success tones and the page banner are derived: not present in the mocks; they follow the system rules.
